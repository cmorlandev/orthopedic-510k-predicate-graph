1. Purpose and why this registry
The goal of preregistration here is narrow and explicit: to fix every analytical decision in advance
so that the results of the 154-code analysis cannot be shaped — consciously or not — by seeing the
numbers first. Concretely, it protects against:
p-hacking — trying multiple thresholds, subsets, or tests and reporting whichever gives the
  cleanest result. Prevented by pre-specifying the exact edge rule, confidence tiers, recall
  linkage, statistical tests, and the full (closed) list of sensitivity analyses in §5–§8.
HARKing (Hypothesizing After Results are Known) — presenting a finding discovered in the data as
  though it had been predicted. Prevented by stating the hypotheses (§4) before the confirmatory
  numbers exist, and by openly disclosing what we already know (§3).
Because this is observational secondary-data research, the appropriate home is an **OSF
Preregistration**, not a trial registry (no intervention) and not PROSPERO (not a systematic review of
health outcomes). OSF's Existing Data template is used specifically because the raw snapshot is
already in hand; that template requires the honesty declaration in §3.

3. Prior-knowledge declaration (required; the honesty core)
This section states exactly what is already known, so that no later claim can be mistaken for a naive
prediction.
3.1 A pilot study has been run. An exploratory analysis on 9 orthopedic product codes (5,426
devices) was completed before this registration. It found, descriptively: ~10% of devices linked to a
recall; recalled devices are cited as predicates by later non-recalled devices; some are first cited
only after their own recall; persistence gaps of up to ~23 years; and higher adverse-event counts
for recalled predicates than matched non-recalled predicates. **These pilot results are known to the
authors and motivate the hypotheses in §4. This work is therefore a pre-registered confirmatory
extension of an exploratory pilot**, not a naive first look.
3.2 The 154-code corpus is a superset of the pilot. The inclusion rule (§4.1) captures all 9 pilot
codes plus 145 more. The pilot devices are thus a subset (~35%) of the 15,556. This is an extension,
not an independent replication; §9 specifies an optional held-out analysis on the 145 non-pilot codes
for cleaner independence.
3.3 What is already computed on the 154-code corpus (descriptive, treated as known):
corpus size: 15,556 unique devices, 13,288 with a Summary;
recall linkage: 1,687 recalled devices (worst-class: 1,305 Class II, 18 Class III, 11 Class I,
  353 unclassified/pre-enforcement).
These are reported as descriptive facts, not as confirmatory tests.
3.4 What is NOT yet computed (the confirmatory quantities this registration locks): the predicate
edge set and its validated precision; all graph-structure metrics; the persistent-recalled-predicate
counts (predicates / citations / downstream devices); first-cited-after-recall count; persistence-gap
distribution; the MAUDE recalled-vs-matched comparison; and every sensitivity subset. **None of these
154-code values existed at the time this plan was registered.**
4. Hypotheses (pre-specified)
Each hypothesis restates a pilot observation as a directional prediction to be tested on the frozen
154-code data. Confirmation/disconfirmation criteria are in §7.
H1 (persistence exists). A non-trivial number of recalled devices are cited as predicates by
  later devices cleared after the recall date, where the citing device is itself not recalled
  ("persistent recalled predicates").
H2 (post-recall entry). A subset of recalled devices are first cited as a predicate only after
  their own recall date.
H3 (long persistence). The maximum interval between a device's recall and its latest post-recall
  predicate citation exceeds 10 years.
H4 (adverse-event association). Recalled predicates have a higher adverse-event burden (MAUDE
  events per year on market) than a matched random sample of non-recalled cited predicates. *This is
  explicitly an associational hypothesis — see §8 and §10; no causal claim is made.*
H5 (robustness). The direction of H1–H4 persists under the pre-specified sensitivity subsets
  (§5), i.e. the findings are not an artifact of low-confidence edges or low-severity recalls.
5. Operational definitions (locked)
Corpus / inclusion rule (D2): every product code in the FDA device classification database with
  medical_specialty = Orthopedic AND device_class = 2 AND implant_flag = Y AND *regulation number
  beginning 888.3*. This yields the 154 codes listed in SNAPSHOT.json. The rule is applied
  mechanically; no code is added or removed by hand.
Predicate cue: a case-insensitive match of predicate, substantially equivalent, or
  reference device in the summary text.
Predicate edge (deterministic rule): in any summary containing a predicate cue, every other
  in-corpus K-number present in the text is recorded as an incoming predicate edge for that device.
  Direction: predicate → citing device.
Confidence tier: SECTION_HEADED if a cue occurs within 300 characters of the K-number's first
  mention; otherwise PROXIMITY_ONLY.
Recalled node: a corpus device whose K-number appears in the structured k_numbers field of a
  recall record. Linkage is by identity only — no fuzzy name matching.
Recall severity class: taken from the enforcement record joined on
  recall.product_res_number = enforcement.recall_number; recalls predating the enforcement dataset
  are labeled n/a and retained, not dropped.
Persistent recalled predicate: a recalled device A such that some device B is cleared in a year
  later than A's recall year, B is a direct successor of A in the graph, and B is not itself recalled.
Downstream device: any B meeting the persistent-predicate condition (counted once).
6. Analysis plan (primary outcomes)
Computed once, on the frozen data, by 06_graph_and_analysis.ipynb, and written to
expected_values.json:
Directed predicate graph over all 15,556 nodes; confirm acyclicity; report node/edge counts,
   connected-node count, largest weakly-connected component, and maximum chain depth.
Primary outcome: count of persistent recalled predicates; number of post-recall citations;
   number of distinct downstream non-recalled devices.
Count of devices first cited as a predicate only after their own recall (H2).
Distribution and maximum of the recall-to-latest-citation gap in years (H3).
Edge-set precision (§8) from a fresh 60-device hand-adjudicated sample.
Coverage diagnostic: % of summaries with a cue, % edge-resolvable, % name-only, % out-of-scope.
7. Confirmation / disconfirmation criteria (pre-specified)
H1 confirmed if persistent recalled predicates &gt; 0 and post-recall citations ≥ 50 (a threshold
  set now, not after seeing the count).
H2 confirmed if first-cited-after-recall count &gt; 0.
H3 confirmed if the maximum recall-to-latest gap &gt; 10 years.
H4 confirmed if the recalled group's median MAUDE events-per-year exceeds the matched group's
  and the pre-specified test (§8) is significant at α = 0.05, one-sided.
H5 confirmed if H1's direction (persistent predicates &gt; 0, citations ≥ 50) holds in all three
  sensitivity subsets (§5.sensitivity below).
Any hypothesis not meeting its criterion is reported as not confirmed — including if that is the
  case. Non-confirmation will be reported with equal prominence.
8. Statistical tests (locked, with multiplicity handling)
Edge precision: proportion of hand-adjudicated candidate edges that are genuine predicate/
  reference citations, with a Wilson 95% confidence interval. Sample: 60 devices drawn with a
  fixed seed (42) from devices with downloaded text. Reported as a precision estimate (adjudication
  of rule-emitted candidates), explicitly not an independent-gold recall estimate.
MAUDE comparison (H4): recalled cited predicates vs. an equal-size random sample (seed 42) of
  non-recalled cited predicates. Primary test: one-sided Mann-Whitney U on events-per-year-on-
  market (time-normalized to remove the age confound). Secondary: two-proportion z-test on the
  match-rate (share with ≥1 event). α = 0.05.
Multiplicity: five hypotheses; H4 is the only inferential test with a p-value, so no correction
  is required for the primary. Where the two MAUDE tests are both reported, both are pre-specified and
  will be reported regardless of outcome.
Sensitivity analyses (closed list — no others will be added post hoc):
Class I/II recalls only (drop Class III and unclassified).
High-confidence edges only (SECTION_HEADED).
Combined (Class I/II recalls AND high-confidence edges).
9. Independence option (extension vs. replication)
Because the 154-code corpus contains the 9-code pilot, the primary analysis is an extension. To offer a
cleaner test, results may additionally be reported on the 145 non-pilot codes only (the devices
not seen in the pilot). This split is pre-specified here so it cannot later be presented as a post-hoc
discovery. It is optional and, if run, will be reported alongside the full-corpus result, not instead
of it.
10. Limitations declared in advance
Name-only gap (D4): ~16% of summaries name a predicate in words but give no K-number. These
  edges cannot be resolved and are not guessed at; the graph is therefore a lower bound on the
  true citation network. This is a known, accepted limitation, reported as such.
MAUDE is passive/voluntary reporting with incomplete device linkage; event counts are a floor,
  and the H4 association may be confounded by device complexity or market volume. No event is
  attributed to the predicate relationship.
No causal claim. The study describes citation structure and its association with recalls and
  adverse events. It does not claim any device is unsafe, nor that predicate lineage causes harm.
Descriptive scope. No manufacturer is named; no device is ranked; no "harm score" is computed.
11. Deviation policy
Any departure from this plan (a definition that has to change, a test that cannot run as written, a
data issue) will be recorded in a DEVIATIONS.md log with the reason and the date, and disclosed in
the manuscript. The frozen expected_values.json (Stage 6 output) and the two-tier verification
(Stage 8) are the immutable record against which the final manuscript numbers are checked.
12. Registration mechanics (sequence)
Register this plan on OSF (Existing-Data preregistration) with the frozen SNAPSHOT.json
   attached, before running Stages 3, 6, 7, 8. This timestamps the analysis plan ahead of the
   confirmatory numbers.
Run Stages 3 → 6 → 7 → 8; Stage 6 writes expected_values.json.
Push code (9 notebooks, verify script, RUN_MANUAL) to a public GitHub repo; the independent
   verifier re-runs and signs off via pull request.
Deposit the full bundle (frozen snapshot + code + results + manuscript) on OSF and mint a DOI; the
   manuscript cites the preregistration and reports any §11 deviations.
